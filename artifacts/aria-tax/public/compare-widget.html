Same income. Three ways to earn it.

The same $100,000 income is taxed very differently depending on how you earn it. Structure changes what you can deduct, which changes what gets taxed, which changes what reaches you.

Employed at a firm

W-2 Employee

$100,000

Gross salary $100,000
Pre-tax benefits 401k, health insurance −$5,000
SS tax (employee half) −$6,200
Medicare (employee half) −$1,450
Federal income tax illustrative −$15,000
State income tax illustrative −$4,000

Estimated take-home

$68,350

  • Employer pays the other half of FICA (7.65%)
  • Benefits and workspace provided by employer
  • Taxes withheld automatically — no quarterly payments
  • No Schedule C, no SE tax
Contracted expert

1099 / Freelance

$100,000

Gross receipts $100,000
Business expenses laptop, software, supplies −$5,000
Net profit $95,000
SE tax (15.3%) both halves of FICA −$13,423
Federal income tax illustrative −$12,500
State income tax illustrative −$3,500

Estimated take-home

$65,577

  • You pay both halves of SE tax — no employer split
  • No employer-provided benefits
  • Nothing withheld — quarterly payments required
  • Income flows through Schedule C → Schedule SE
Solo practice

Small Business

$100,000

Gross revenue $100,000
Business expenses office, software, insurance, marketing −$25,000
Net profit $75,000
SE tax (15.3%) applied to net profit −$10,597
Federal income tax illustrative −$9,500
State income tax illustrative −$2,600

Estimated take-home

$52,303

  • Real overhead is deductible — lowers the taxable base
  • Higher expenses = lower net profit = lower SE tax
  • SE tax applies to net profit, not gross revenue
  • Quarterly payments required
